Chapter 034 — The Inquisition

A comprehensive exploration of the shadowy and complex force that polices, purges, and protects the Imperium from the shadows: the Inquisition. This guide examines their origins, mandate, organization, dogmas, and the internal schisms that have shaped their infamous legacy.

2026-11-21RIFTBOUND Editorial10 MIN READ

Shadows Over Gethsemane Hive

In the flickering half-light of Gethsemane Hive, an Inquisitor’s sigil opens doors upon which others dare not knock. Here, a council of planetary leaders quakes beneath the silent scrutiny of a figure cloaked in black, his rosette unmistakable. Across the Imperium, such scenes echo as the Inquisition’s gaze turns toward suspected heresy, xenos influence, or daemonic corruption. With one word, this shadowy figure may condemn a world or save it from hidden threats. The Inquisition’s power extends beyond armies, courts, and even planetary governors, forming the secret linchpin of Imperial survival.

The Inquisition is often described as the Imperium’s last and most ruthless defense. Many new to Warhammer 40K first encounter its symbol—the burnished 'I'—atop forbidden tomes or in the whispers of terrified bureaucrats. Unlike the vast armadas or hulking Space Marine Chapters, the Inquisition operates invisibly, its members employing subterfuge as often as force. Each Inquisitor answers only to their own conscience and, in theory, to the Emperor Himself. This concentration of authority breeds both astounding success and notorious abuses.

As we begin our journey, consider the Inquisition not as a monolith, but as a shifting coalition of ideologues and pragmatic survivors. Their origins, roles, and notorious methods reveal the perpetual conflict inherent to humanity’s struggle in the bleak far future. Next, we delve into the fires that forged the Inquisition—tracing their roots all the way to the Horus Heresy and the collapse of mankind’s golden age.

Origins in the Horus Heresy

The Inquisition’s creation is intimately tied to the greatest disaster in human history: the Horus Heresy. As traitorous Space Marines threw the Imperium into civil war, the Emperor’s most loyal servants recognized that survival required secrecy and unyielding devotion. According to canonical lore, Malcador the Sigillite and select Primarchs laid the conceptual groundwork for what would become the Inquisition amidst the ashes of the Traitor Legions.

This fledgling body, intended to root out heresy and chaos, was formalized in the first centuries following the Heresy’s end. Throughout the Scouring—the Imperium’s bloody campaign of retribution—the newly anointed Inquisitors inherited secret powers and privileges, far beyond those of most Imperial authorities. Their earliest recorded deeds told of rooting out hidden cults, silencing dangerous knowledge, and interrogating even Astartes when suspicion demanded it.

Uncertainty persists regarding the exact timeline and individuals involved in these earliest days. Some texts credit Malcador explicitly, while others suggest that the Inquisition was shaped piecemeal by survivors of many factions. Regardless, by M32 the organization’s influence was undeniable, and its most feared traditions—secrecy, autonomy, and the legendary Exterminatus—were already fully established. This uncertain birth gave rise to organizational fluidity that persists to the present era.

Mandate: To Protect by Any Means

The Inquisition’s remit is famously broad, its methods unrestricted by conventional law. At its core lies a single, terrible mandate: defend the Imperium from all threats, no matter the cost. To fulfill this charge, Inquisitors can requisition resources, overrule planetary governors, and seize command of Imperial Guard regiments—or act alone in utter secrecy. No threat is considered too small or too great: from Chaos cults festering within a manufactorum to xenos infiltration of Imperial courts.

Their chief weapon is absolute autonomy. Unlike formal militaries or Adeptus Administratum officials bound by rules, Inquisitors operate as judge, jury, and executioner. Their power of Exterminatus—the annihilation of entire planets—remains the most infamous example of their reach. Such acts, though rare, showcase the dire stakes they face. Most operate in subtler ways, investigating threats in isolation, eliminating or recruiting allies as needed.

The boundaries of Inquisitorial mandate have always been debated. Some question whether any mortal should wield such unchecked authority, while others see it as the only rational response facing a galaxy overrun by daemons, xenos, and traitors. Official doctrine and legendary cases illuminate this dilemma: the Inquisition’s successes and horrors alike stem from its sweeping, sometimes ambiguous charge.

The Three Great Ordos

Despite popular misconception, the Inquisition isn’t a single, faceless bureaucracy. Rather, it is composed of three main divisions, or 'Ordos', each formed to counter a fundamental threat. The Ordo Hereticus roots out corruption and witchcraft within the Imperium, the Ordo Malleus battles the daemonic, and the Ordo Xenos confronts alien infiltration and technology. Each Ordo cultivates its own traditions, specialist forces, and dogmas.

The Ordo Hereticus, the 'Witch Hunters,' typically focuses on keeping humanity pure from within. They spearhead purges against rogue psykers, mutant outbreaks, and heretical cults. Ordo Malleus Inquisitors, by contrast, are expert daemonhunters, often wielding arcane weaponry and leading the elite Grey Knights Chapter. Ordo Xenos, meanwhile, is famous (or infamous) for its pragmatic, sometimes dangerously radical approach to non-human contact.

While these three Ordos dominate the Inquisitional structure, many minor Ordos exist, spun out to address emergent threats or splinter ideologies. Ordo Sicarius, Ordo Chronos, and others highlight the organization’s adaptability and sometimes chaotic expansion. Understanding the distinctions and rivalries between these Ordos is crucial to grasping the complex politics at the heart of the Inquisition.

Puritan vs Radical: The Inquisition Divided

No discussion of the Inquisition is complete without acknowledging its deepest and most dangerous schism: Puritan versus Radical. These ideological camps define how Inquisitors interpret their mandate, employ forbidden knowledge, and even judge each other. Puritans favor strict orthodoxy—using only the purest tools and methods sanctioned by centuries of tradition. They see any traffic with xenos technology or Chaos as anathema.

Radicals, however, argue that to defeat the Imperium’s enemies, one must occasionally use their weapons against them. They might employ tainted relics, consort with sanctioned xenos, or even recruit repentant heretics. This approach breeds results—but also breeds suspicion, corruption, and witch-hunts within the Inquisition itself. Famous Radicals have achieved great victories before being executed by their peers for heretical overreach.

This internal conflict manifests not only in philosophical debates but in open strife, betrayal, and clandestine cells. The volatile equilibrium between Puritans and Radicals has repeatedly shaped Imperial history, sometimes tipping entire sectors into civil war. Throughout the millennia, neither side has ever fully triumphed, ensuring the Inquisition remains both effective and dangerously unpredictable.

Inquisitorial Tools, Agents, and Retinues

An Inquisitor rarely works alone. While the power of the Rosette grants access to almost any resource, most Inquisitors build skilled retinues of acolytes, Interrogators, sanctioned psykers, and more. These agents serve as eyes, muscle, and mind—helping the Inquisitor navigate unique threats or local politics. Some retinues resemble small private armies, while others cloak themselves as simple scholars or mercenaries.

Iconic among these are the famously loyal henchmen: daemonhosts (used only by the most Radical), chirurgeons, Jokaero weaponsmiths, Crusaders, and Death Cult Assassins. Each brings specialized skills and arcane knowledge. Retinues are often assembled, reshaped, or sacrificed as the mission demands. This diversity reflects the unpredictable nature of Inquisitorial missions: subtle investigation one day, sanctioned violence the next.

More secretive still are the 'Inquisitors Extraordinary'—Grey Knights, Officio Assassinorum operatives, and other elite Imperial tools, called upon only rarely. The careful, ruthless management of these assets can mean victory over insidious enemies—or the escalation of entire planetary crises. Studying these entourages reveals the breadth of the Inquisition’s capabilities and their high cost in loyalty and lives.

Legendary Inquisitors and Precedents

As with any secretive organization, the Inquisition’s history is shaped by outsized personalities: Torquemada Coteaz, Gregor Eisenhorn, Gideon Ravenor, and others have become cautionary legends. Eisenhorn’s descent from implacable Puritanism into Radical compromise is especially well-documented, illustrating how the Inquisition’s own goals can corrupt even its most devoted. Coteaz embodies the extreme orthodoxy of the Ordo Malleus, famous for wielding the Exterminatus order as a blunt solution.

These legendary Inquisitors have not only changed the fate of worlds, they’ve reshaped Imperial policy. Ravenor continued Eisenhorn’s controversial methods but added unorthodox telepathy and bioengineering. Some Inquisitors, such as Kryptman of the Ordo Xenos, have been held responsible for genocidal tactics against entire star systems, reflecting the terrifying scale of the Inquisition’s actions.

Yet, no two Inquisitors are ever truly alike. Each balances mission, conscience, and Imperium’s demands in isolation, accountable only to their peers—when they can be found—and the shadow of the Emperor’s will. Their deeds both inspire loyalty and stoke rebellion within and outside the Inquisition. Understanding these individual stories is key to grasping why the Inquisition’s legend endures.

Exterminatus: Last Resort and Infamy

Few words in the Warhammer 40,000 universe inspire more dread than 'Exterminatus.' This ultimate sanction ensures no threat—be it daemonic infestation or irredeemable heresy—can spread. Approved only by senior Inquisitors, Exterminatus involves virus bombs, cyclonic torpedoes, or orbital bombardment, rendering a planet lifeless in minutes. Official lore details dozens of infamous cases, each refueling the debate over necessity versus atrocity.

The procedure for authorizing Exterminatus is shrouded in ceremony and risk. An Inquisitor’s authority, combined with irrefutable evidence, is required. The consequences extend beyond mere destruction—generational trauma and posthumous condemnation follow, creating martyrs or monstrous examples. Some Inquisitors, such as Kryptman, have ordered Exterminatus multiple times, labeling them heroes—or genocidal fanatics.

Controversy surrounds the doctrine: is such power compatible with the Imperium’s stated values, or proof of its decay? Even other Imperial institutions, from Space Marines to Administratum officials, occasionally balk at the Inquisition’s willingness to turn Exterminatus against civilian populations. The chilling reality: the mere threat of Exterminatus empowers Inquisitors as much as its execution.

Inquisition and the Imperial Machine

Officially, the Inquisition stands outside the Imperium’s labyrinthine bureaucracy. In practice, its mandate frequently clashes with the Adeptus Mechanicus, Astra Militarum, Ecclesiarchy, and Space Marine Chapters. Conflict arises when Inquisitorial investigations disrupt planetary tithes, contaminate sacred relics, or call martial resources for secret wars. The Inquisition’s need for secrecy and autonomy fosters both resentment and dependence.

Nevertheless, some Imperial organizations, such as the Grey Knights or Adepta Sororitas, maintain direct ties of service or rivalry. The Inquisition alone can authorize Grey Knights deployments beyond standard daemon-warfare, cementing their alliance. Conversely, the Ecclesiarchy views the Inquisition as both a necessary purifier and a threat to its own spiritual monopoly.

This delicate balance sometimes erupts into open conflict—Inquisitorial Purges of Mechanicus planets, compulsory recruitment from Astra Militarum ranks, and interference in Inquisitorial affairs by the High Lords or even Space Marines. The Inquisition remains a vital, controversial engine driving the Imperium’s endless war for survival. This relationship remains ever tense, a hallmark of 40K’s layered setting.

The Inquisition Across Eras and Media

Interpretations of the Inquisition have evolved since the earliest editions of Warhammer 40,000, adapting to shifts in lore and tone. Early depictions (late 1980s–early 1990s) favored shadowy, almost monastic figures wielding ancient relics and hidden authority. Over time, Inquisitors became more individualized, their rivalries and radical/puritan conflict highlighted in the works of authors like Dan Abnett.

Modern interpretations emphasize the Inquisition’s internal politics, complex moral ambiguities, and the sheer scope of their operations. Official sources and licensed games often focus on iconic Inquisitors—allowing for both heroic and monstrous portrayals, depending on the story’s needs. While tabletop miniatures support the grimdark aesthetic, novels and games like 'Inquisitor' and 'Battlefleet Gothic' expand on backroom intrigue and doomed investigations.

Uncertainty (and debate) persists regarding key aspects: the total number of Inquisitors, the frequency of Exterminatus, and the Inquisition’s overall effectiveness. Canonical contradictions reflect the murky, contradictory nature of the Imperium itself. For both veterans and newcomers, understanding these nuances is essential for appreciating the Inquisition’s lasting appeal.

The Inquisition’s Legacy and Influence

The Inquisition casts a long shadow over the Imperium’s lore—both as a necessary evil and a source of existential dread. Its blend of absolute autonomy, moral ambiguity, and internal rivalry have made it a storytelling cornerstone. Many of 40K’s most compelling tales—investigating daemon cults, purging worlds, betraying allies—revolve around Inquisitorial edicts. These stories echo themes of corruption and redemption.

In-game, the Inquisition appears as a versatile force—sometimes playable, sometimes an outside threat. Campaigns and narrative events leverage its capacity for secret missions, special operations, and world-changing decrees. Independent writers and fans continue to explore its depths, generating an endless trove of personal campaigns, characters, and what-if scenarios.

This enduring legacy is double-edged: the Inquisition is both protector and potential destroyer, inspiring admiration and terror in equal measure. As the galaxy darkens in the Era Indomitus, their actions may yet define whether the Imperium survives another millennium—or succumbs to the very corruption it fights.
