Chapter 006 — The Necrons

From the nightmarish depths of ancient tomb worlds, the Necrons emerge—immortal, remorseless, and relentless in their march across the galaxy. This guide dissects their mysterious origins, fractured dynasties, and chilling ambitions in the Warhammer 40,000 universe.

2026-10-24RIFTBOUND Editorial10 MIN READ

Awakening in the Black Dawn

A silent plain beneath a dying sun erupts as cyclopean monoliths shudder and ascend. From cavernous tombs carved beneath the world’s surface, metallic forms stir—cold, unfeeling, implacable. This is the awakening of the Necrons, a moment that signals doom for any life above. Across the galaxy, similar risings occur, each a prelude to devastation as these undying legions reclaim their lost domains. The galaxy, already rent by ceaseless war, faces an ancient foe it scarcely remembers.

For those new to the Warhammer 40,000 universe, the Necrons are one of its most enigmatic and terrifying xenos factions. Once a living species plagued by frailty and bitterness, they exchanged flesh for immortality—at a terrible price. That transformation, and the epochs-long slumber that followed, set them in stark contrast to other factions. Understanding the Necrons means exploring not just another alien race, but a tragedy played out across millions of years.

Even among long-term fans, the Necrons’ awakening has undergone reinterpretation over the decades. Older iterations cast them as silent, mindless harvesters. More recent canon restores their ancient intellect and complex hierarchies. Variations aside, their emergence always signals catastrophe for the present-day Imperium, their motives wrapped in chilly inscrutability. The first encounter is rarely the last—a promise of further revelations still buried in stasis-locked vaults.

Flesh to Metal: Origins of the Necron Race

Long before mankind lit its first fire, the Necrontyr gazed bitterly at a universe indifferent to their suffering. Their sun’s radiation stole years from their lifespans, stoking resentment and ambition. Gripped by a culture obsessed with legacy, they warred endlessly, unable to escape the shackles of mortality. Their scientists and leaders probed cosmic mysteries in search of deliverance, setting the greatest tragedy in motion.

In desperation, the Necrontyr struck an alliance with the C’tan—star gods of unimaginable power but alien appetites. The C’tan offered immortality, but the bargain was a cruel deception. Driven by hunger, these cosmic entities shepherded the Necrontyr through biotransference, sloughing away their flesh for necrodermis shells and downloading souls into machines. The dawn of the Necrons extinguished the old species—leaving only eternal, hollow warriors.

The trauma of this transformation lies at the core of Necron lore. It explains not just their chilling appearance, but their fractured culture and relentless, almost robotic, drive. While canon is consistent regarding these events, the true extent of the Necrontyr’s complicity—and the nuances of their fall—are sometimes left intentionally vague, inviting further contemplation or exploration in new editions.

The War in Heaven: Divinities and Cataclysm

Newly forged, the Necrons joined their C’tan overlords in a war that reshaped reality itself—the War in Heaven. Their adversaries were the Old Ones, ancient beings who had shepherded the first races of the galaxy. What started as a desire for revenge cascaded into aeonic carnage, introducing sorcery and technology on such a scale that the laws of physics bent under their onslaught.

Ultimately, the C’tan’s predations grew unbearable. The Necrons revolted, shattering these star gods into shards and imprisoning them. Victorious, but vastly depleted, the Necrons found their ancient enemies gone and the galaxy thick with nascent powers. This act of rebellion, paradoxically, imprisoned the Necrons in a cycle of betrayal—a poignant echo of their own origin story.

Accounts of the War in Heaven have developed over time. Early sources hinted at the C’tan’s omnipotence; later lore clarifies their vulnerabilities. The conflict’s exact duration and scope remain ambiguous, lending it a mythic resonance. What’s certain is that this conflict left drifts of legend, terrible weapons, and psychic scars across the galaxy—remnants that even the Necrons themselves sometimes struggle to remember.

The Long Sleep: Tomb Worlds and Stasis

Exhausted by war, the Necrons retreated to their innumerable tomb worlds, switching their minds into dormancy while automated guardians waited in silence. For over sixty million years, vast crypt-complexes burrowed beneath dead or barren planets kept their masters safe against the tides of galactic change. It is an image both morbid and awe-inspiring: a sleeping dynasty beneath the surface, indifferent to the empires that flourished and fell above.

Tomb Worlds themselves are marvels of Necron engineering—self-repairing, shielded fortresses, littered with traps and cryptic mechanisms. Legends persist of explorers happening upon such sites, only to vanish or emerge haunted. The long sleep is punctuated by rare technical malfunctions, creating cursed domains where Necron forces function with fractured or aberrant programming—adding unpredictability to their awakening.

Interpretations of the long sleep reflect shifts in how Games Workshop approaches Necron lore. Early versions depicted the Necrons as single-minded automatons, but recent editions reintroduce their individual personalities and dynastic rivalries. However, the stasis endured by many offers plot hooks for future canonical developments—a reminder that within the crypt, uncertainties and surprises abound.

Dynastic Rule: The Necron Empire Reborn

The awakening of the Necrons was not uniform. Some tomb worlds roused earlier, some remained lost. Each dynasty confronted a changed galaxy, ruled by different Phaerons (Necron overlords) with ambitions as vast and diverse as the stars. These dynasties—like the Sautekh, Mephrit, and Nihilakh—reign as independent, often fractious sovereigns, each with their own heraldry, customs, and stratagems.

At the core of each dynasty is a Phaeron, attended by a Royal Court of trusted lieutenants: Crypteks (arcane engineers), Overlords (warlords), and Lords (provincial commanders). These individuals preserve vanity, obsession, and memory from their organic origins, leading to internal intrigue and bitter rivalries. Some, like Imotekh the Stormlord of the Sautekh, seek galactic domination. Others are content to reclaim their lost territory or pursue inscrutable plans.

This structure contributes to the internal tensions and narrative possibilities of Necron-focused tales. Canon has evolved: early depictions were monolithic, but now, dynastic politics reflect the diversity and depth found in other factions. The result is a tapestry of motivations and methods, each dynasty’s ambitions colored by the traumas and traditions carried from deep time.

Mechanisms of Immortality: Technology and Warfare

Necron armies march to war mounted on anti-gravitic skimmers, wielding weapons powered by arcane energy. At their core are the relentless Warriors—a phalanx of undying soldiers. More specialized units, such as Lychguard (royal protectors), Canoptek constructs, and terrifying Monoliths, provide versatility and terror on the battlefield. Necron technology blurs the line between science and sorcery, relying on principles inscrutable even to their oldest foes.

Self-repair is fundamental to the Necrons. Their bodies, woven from living metal (necrodermis), can reconstruct after nearly any wound. This, combined with teleportation-based weapons (Gauss technology) and reanimation protocols, makes their defeat unnervingly temporary. Even vaporized Necron warriors can sometimes return, their forms slowly knitting together in distant tombs.

These technological marvels often outstrip the understanding of modern Imperial scientists. Some devices appear almost magical, drawing on science lost to the galaxy’s current civilizations—leaving Necron victories to be regarded as miracles or curses. Each new Codex introduces refinements to these machines, drawing clearer lines between unit roles, and expanding the arsenal at the dynasty’s disposal.

The C’tan Shards: Star Gods in Chains

Today, the C’tan are no longer free. The Necrons, having shattered their former masters into discrete shards, now weaponize these god-fragments in battle. C’tan shards are contained within elaborate tesseract labyrinths, released only when their destructive power is needed. Each one is a paradox: a deity bound in service, both prisoner and doomsday weapon.

The most infamous C’tan include the Nightbringer, Deceiver, and Void Dragon—each with unique personalities and manifestations. In battle, a C’tan Shard’s abilities defy rational expectation: warping time, unleashing stellar fire, or manipulating fate itself. Yet their captivity is never guaranteed; a C’tan’s fragmentary awareness and capricious nature often create as much peril for its Necron minders as for the enemy.

Lore around the C’tan and their shards often sparks debate. Old sources presented them as true gods; newer canon clarifies that even gods can be broken. The narrative implications are profound—a motif of power turned inward, and a warning that ancient tools can still carry an unpredictable cost.

The Pariahs and Flayed Ones: Shadows of Damnation

Not all Necrons slumber or serve with dignity. The Flayed Ones are an aberrant offshoot: Necrons afflicted by a horrible curse from the C’tan known as the Flayer. Their programmed minds are shattered; they obsessively flense organic victims and drape themselves in fresh skin. On the battlefield, their horror is as much psychological as physical, for they move with maddened hunger, clawing and gibbering silently.

The concept of the Pariah has shifted in the setting. Once described as invasive human-Necron hybrids, the Pariah trait now refers to beings who radiate a psychic null field, erasing presence and unnerving all psykers. The Necrons utilize such technology to field Lychguard with Pariah traits, and legends persist that this quality may be further weaponized or woven into cryptic dynastic rituals.

Independent resources and new codices highlight how aberrant subgroups like the Flayed Ones illustrate the price of Necron immortality. Their very existence emphasizes the unpredictable aftermath of biotransference—innovative biological meddling gone awry. As the faction’s background grows, so does the implication that even in technological perfection, flaws and curses are eternally waiting.

Personalities of the Tomb: Named Characters

Necron dynasties are not just armies of identical drones; they are peopled by titanic personalities. Perhaps the best-known is Imotekh the Stormlord, Phaeron of the Sautekh. A master tactician, Imotekh wields storms both metaphorical and real against his foes, outmaneuvering entire Imperial campaigns. His relentless ambition and strategic brilliance make him a star example of Necron leadership in modern lore.

Other icons include Trazyn the Infinite, a collector whose hunger for rare artifacts—and living specimens—places him both inside and outside Necron politics. As both preserver and thief, Trazyn’s endless galleries hold moments of past and present, including trophies pilfered from the Imperium and other xenos. These characters, with their feuds, obsessions, and foibles, underscore the restored individuality Games Workshop has woven back into Necron canon.

From Nemesor Zahndrekh, who fights wars from the perspective of a vanished era, to Orikan the Diviner, who manipulates time itself, the growing cast of Necron personalities moves beyond the stereotype of emotionless automatons. Each new codex expands the nuance and scale of tomb world politics, offering narrative hooks for campaign play and drama among the undying.

Necrons and the Modern Galaxy

As more tomb worlds awaken, the Necrons wield increasing influence over the galactic narrative. Their reappearance shocks both the Imperium and other xenos; few remember their history, and fewer still survive contact. In the current era, dynasties chart expansionist campaigns, fight among themselves, and occasionally form brief, uneasy alliances with other powers, further complicating the already unstable setting of Warhammer 40,000.

Necron technology and schemes shape many conflicts. Whether scavenging for Blackstone (a rare psychoactive mineral) or fending off Tyranid incursions, the Necrons are both predators and enigmatic guardians. At times, they appear to pursue goals that defy mortal understanding—rescuing planets from daemonic threats or manipulating warp storms. Their inscrutability ensures that every Necron invasion is shrouded in uncertainty, and victory often comes at a hidden price.

Official lore and independent guides both highlight how the Necrons evolve with each new edition. Their storylines mesh with larger setting changes—the arrival of the Great Rift, the rise of the Primaris Space Marines, and the ongoing tension with Chaos and the T’au. This dynamic ensures the Necrons will remain central, their secrets and ambitions ready to resurface as new threats emerge.

The Future of the Legions: Unanswered Questions

Thousands of tomb worlds have yet to wake, and throughout the galaxy, Necron monarchs debate how to proceed: isolated vengeance, dynastic cooperation, or conquest? The long slumber has left records in ruins and lines of succession blurred, leading to unresolved plots. Intrigue, betrayal, and the possibility of discovering still more potent relics promises that the story of the Necrons is far from over.

The fate of the C’tan, the undiscovered tomb worlds, and the cryptic relationship between Necron technology and warp phenomena are all topics that continue to inspire debate and development. For all their immortal precision, the Necrons face uncertainty on a cosmic scale. Some lore suggests the potential for even further evolution—perhaps a reclamation of lost emotion, or a final reckoning with their creators and betrayers.

For the modern hobbyist or lore-enthusiast, the Necrons offer a rare blend of horror, nostalgia, and speculative science fiction. Their journey from vanishing mortals to mechanical legions, and the philosophical implications of identity and legacy, remain open-ended—teasing both players and readers with new mysteries, even as new battles erupt on ancient, starlit tomb worlds.
